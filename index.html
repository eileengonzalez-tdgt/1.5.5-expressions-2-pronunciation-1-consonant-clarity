Pronunciation 1 | Consonant Clarity

Learning objective: Distinguish and produce /θ/, /ð/, /v/ and /b/ in words and familiar conversational phrases, improving intelligibility in everyday and customer-service situations.

01 · Encounter: Can You Hear the Difference?

10 minutes · Listen first. Do not look at the spelling until you have made your choice.

Discuss

  1. Which pair was most difficult to distinguish?
  2. What changed: tongue position, lip position, or voice vibration?
  3. How could confusing these sounds affect a phone conversation?

02 · Clarify: The Consonant Sound Lab

20 minutes · Watch, notice mouth position, repeat, then practice the target sound in a sentence.

Video 1 · /θ/ and /ð/

TH: Voiceless and Voiced

/θ/ (think, thank): place your tongue gently between your teeth and let air pass. No voice vibration. /ð/ (this, they): keep a similar position and use your voice.

▶ Watch TH Pronunciation ↗
Pause & repeat: think · thank · three | this · that · they
Say it in context: “Thank you for helping me with this.”
Video 2 · /v/

V: Teeth + Lower Lip

Touch your upper teeth lightly to your lower lip. Let air flow continuously and use your voice. Unlike /b/, your lips do not close together.

▶ Watch V Pronunciation ↗
Pause & repeat: very · verify · voice · review
Say it in context: “Let me verify that information for you.”
Video 3 · /b/

B: Both Lips Close

Close both lips briefly, build air pressure, then release. Compare the stop in /b/ with the continuous airflow of /v/.

▶ Watch B Pronunciation ↗
Pause & repeat: bill · balance · better · before
Say it in context: “I believe there is a problem with my bill.”
Compare: very / berry · vest / best · they / day · think / tink. Listen to the first sound, then repeat with accurate mouth placement. The final pair includes a nonstandard pronunciation of think for contrast.

03 · Remember: Sound Boxes

10 minutes · Whole-class repetition. Select any word tile to highlight it and hear a model pronunciation when supported by the browser.

Selected word: Choose a tile to begin

Trainer-led practice

  1. Model & repeat (4 min): Say each word clearly; learners repeat twice.
  2. Contrast challenge (3 min): Alternate /θ/ with /ð/, then /v/ with /b/.
  3. Random challenge (3 min): Use Shuffle or select tiles out of order. Learners identify the sound and pronounce the word.
Pronunciation reminder: The objective is audible sound production. There is no written score for this stage. Give immediate oral feedback and invite another attempt.

04 · Internalize: Pronunciation Clinic

15 minutes · Work in pairs. Read each conversation with natural pacing, identify difficult sounds, and repeat after peer feedback. Then switch roles.

Dialogue A · Everyday

A group project that needs a better plan

Maya: Thanks for meeting me before class. I think there's a problem with our group presentation, and I'd really value your opinion.

Ben: Of course. What's bothering you? I thought we had everything ready for Thursday.

Maya: We have the information, but the examples aren't very clear. I'm worried the other students won't understand our main point.

Ben: That's a fair concern. Could you show me the section you think needs improvement?

Maya: Sure. This is the part about the benefits of volunteering. I believe we need a better example from everyday life.

Ben: I agree. What if we include the story about helping at the community event? That would make the message more believable.

Maya: That's a great suggestion. Would you be available this evening to review the slides with me?

Ben: Yes, after seven. We can divide the work and check everything together.

Maya: Perfect. Thank you for being so helpful. I feel much better about the presentation now.

Ben: You're welcome. Let's make sure both of us feel confident before Thursday.

Pronunciation focus: thanks, think, Thursday, thought, this, that, very, value, volunteering, believe, better, benefits, both.
Speaking extension: Replace one suggestion with your own idea and respond naturally.
Dialogue B · BPO

Investigating an unexpected charge

Customer: Good morning. I'm calling because I believe there's a problem with my latest bill. I've been charged for a service I never requested.

Agent: Thank you for explaining the situation. I understand why that would be frustrating, especially if the charge was unexpected. I'll be happy to review it with you.

Customer: I contacted support before, but I haven't received a clear answer. I'd really like to resolve this today.

Agent: I understand, and I appreciate your patience. Before I review the billing details, could you please verify the name and email address associated with your account?

Customer: Yes, of course. The name is Daniel Rivera, and the email address is the one on my account profile.

Agent: Thank you. I've located the account. I can see an additional service charge from last Thursday. Let me check when that service was activated.

Customer: That's the charge I'm talking about. I don't believe I approved anything extra.

Agent: I see why you're concerned. Based on what I can verify, the charge needs further review. I can submit a billing dispute and provide a reference number so you can follow the progress.

Customer: That sounds better. How long will the review take?

Agent: The billing team will confirm the expected timeframe. I don't want to give you an inaccurate estimate. Would you like me to explain the next steps?

Customer: Yes, please. Thank you for taking the time to explain everything clearly.

Agent: You're very welcome. I'll summarize the issue and the next steps before we finish, so we're both on the same page.

Pronunciation focus: believe, bill, been, before, verify, review, service, Thursday, that, this, thank, both.
Speaking extension: The customer asks one unexpected follow-up question. The agent responds without reading.

Peer coaching checklist

Feedback frame: “Your ___ sound was clear. Let's try ___ again. Watch your tongue/lips.” Focus on a small number of sounds rather than interrupting every sentence.

05 · Fluent Use: Group Communication Challenge

10 minutes · Work in groups of 3–4. Each group opens its own scenario, prepares a natural conversation, and presents it to the class.

Group instructions: Assign a speaking role to every member. Use expressions practiced earlier this week, but create your own conversation. Include several examples of TH (/θ/, /ð/) and V/B (/v/, /b/) sounds. Do not read a fully scripted dialogue during the presentation.

Choose your group

Each button reveals a different scenario. Open only your group's card while preparing.

Planning a volunteer event

Situation: Your team is organizing a community volunteer event, but the venue has changed at the last minute. Some volunteers haven't received the update.

Roles: Event coordinator, volunteer, venue representative; optional fourth role: another volunteer.

Challenge: Clarify the change, explain the problem, suggest a workable plan, and thank the people helping.

Sound targets: think, this, Thursday, volunteers, venue, available, better, both.

Billing support escalation

Situation: A customer sees an unfamiliar charge and has already contacted support. The agent needs help from a supervisor before explaining the next steps.

Roles: Customer, support agent, supervisor; optional fourth role: billing specialist.

Challenge: Verify details, acknowledge frustration, explain what can be reviewed, and close with a clear next step. Do not promise an unconfirmed refund.

Sound targets: bill, believe, verify, review, this, that, thank, service.

A difficult team decision

Situation: Your workplace team must deliver a presentation, but two colleagues disagree about the best solution and the deadline is approaching.

Roles: Team leader, colleague A, colleague B; optional fourth role: project stakeholder.

Challenge: Invite both opinions, clarify the disagreement, propose a compromise, and confirm responsibilities.

Sound targets: think, they, together, very, value, believe, better, both.

Helping a visitor with a reservation

Situation: A visitor arrives at a hotel with a reservation, but the booking details don't match the information in the system.

Roles: Visitor, front-desk agent, booking support representative; optional fourth role: manager.

Challenge: Ask polite questions, verify the booking, explain available options, and reach a respectful resolution.

Sound targets: this, there, thank, verify, available, visitor, booking, better.

Prepare and present

  1. Prepare (3 min): Agree on roles, key information, and a logical beginning, middle, and ending. Every student must speak.
  2. Present (5–6 min): Groups perform short conversations for the class. Aim for 45–60 seconds per group; the trainer may adjust the number of presentations to class size.
  3. Reflect (1–2 min): Complete the self-assessment individually after presenting or observing.
